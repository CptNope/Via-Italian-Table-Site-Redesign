Affissioni

VIA's identity is a billposting wall on Shrewsbury Street: flat, colour-drenched Italian posters, each one dated, pasted up when something is on and pasted over when it ends.

VIA Italian Table · Brand book · Proposal, October 2026

The idea

Why posters, and why it fits VIA.

Most restaurants show a dark room, a plate, and a gold serif. VIA has something they don't: a constant run of dated things to come for. PigFest, winemaker dinners, the suckling pig, the turkey kit, the $25 list. The poster wall turns that calendar into the brand.

Italian

Mid-century Italian poster craft (Depero, Olivetti, Pirelli): flat colour, big type, cut-paper shapes.

Street

VIA means street. The affissioni wall is street furniture, and VIA lives on Worcester's restaurant row.

Current

Every poster carries its dates. When a date passes, the poster comes down. Nothing on the site is ever stale.

Logo: two options

VIA chooses. Both work inside the same poster system; the site can switch between them from the bar at the top.

Option A · Refresh
VIAItalian Table

Keep the gold mark

VIA's existing Roman-capital mark stays exactly as it is; only the world around it changes. Lowest risk, and it keeps the recognition VIA has already built. Shown here redrawn in Cinzel as a stand-in: final files come from VIA's master artwork.

  • Always on ink or plum; the gold needs a dark ground.
  • Posters carry the colour; the mark stays gold and small.
Option B · Rebrand
Italian Table

The cut-paper VIA

Three letters cut from paper, with a saffron sun rising inside the A: the fire under the spit, the arch of a street, the start of an evening. Built from straight cuts and one arc, so it reproduces at any size, in one colour, on any poster.

  • The sun is always saffron, or knocked out in one-colour use.
  • Works in ink, paper, or any poster colour with 4.5:1 contrast.

Option B in use

Clear space and size

Keep clear space equal to the height of the sun on every side. Smallest size: 64px wide on screen, 18mm in print. Below that, use "VIA" set in Archivo Black.

Don't

  • Stretch, outline, or add a shadow to the mark.
  • Put the sun in any colour other than saffron (or brick on a saffron ground).
  • Set "VIA" in another face and call it the logo.
  • Place the mark over a busy photo; give it a poster field.
  • Mix Option A and Option B in one piece.

Cut-paper shapes

Six shapes, cut from the poster colours. Each stands for something real at VIA. They live in a poster's art band, never behind running text.

  • Flat fills only. No gradients, outlines or textures.
  • Shapes may overlap and bleed off the poster's edge, like paper trimmed after pasting.
  • One composition per poster; two or three shapes at most.

The poster

Every offer, event and special is one poster, built the same way. A poster with no end date says so ("for a limited time", "any night").

RegisterAsk about it
  1. Art band. The cut-paper composition, at the top, clear of text.
  2. Dates. Bold start or end date first, then what kind of thing it is.
  3. Title. Poster type: the name of the thing, not a slogan.
  4. Body. One or two plain sentences, and real dishes with real prices when there are any.
  5. Tear-off tabs. One to three actions, cut like a street flyer: Book, Call, Register, See the menu. On the site the tab tears as you press it.

Photography

Photos are pasted onto posters like a clipping, slightly tilted, never full-bleed behind type. The site has labeled slots ready for VIA's own pictures.

  • Real service, real light: shoot during dinner, not on a cleared set.
  • Hands, steam, flame, the gelato case, the spit turning.
  • Tight crops of one dish, overhead or at table height.
  • Dark moody plates on black slate.
  • Stock "Italian" props: checked cloths, Chianti flasks.
  • Text set over a photo.

Voice

Warm, plain and specific, like a server who knows the menu. Say the dish, the date, the price, the room size.
